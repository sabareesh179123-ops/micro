02 // Problem Definition & UN Alignment

The Invisible Microplastic Crisis & EV Acceleration

While exhaust pipes draw policy regulation, tire wear remains the single largest source of aquatic microplastic pollution worldwide.

28% Aquatic Pollution

High-shear friction between roads and tires breaks synthetic rubber into micro-particles. Rain washes these tire particles into rivers and oceans, comprising up to 28% of all primary microplastics in waterways.

Over 6,000,000 tons shed globally per year.

EV Heavy Torque Degradation

Electric Vehicles (EVs) are 20% to 30% heavier due to battery packs and produce instantaneous torque. This accelerates tire wear by 20–50% compared to traditional internal combustion engine vehicles.

Unreinforced tires fail rapidly under EV loads.

6PPD-Quinone Toxic Runoff

Tires use 6PPD as an ozone preservative. When exposed to air, it reacts into 6PPD-quinone, a chemical compound washed off during driving that causes massive aquatic mortality in freshwater species.

Micro-Rubber Zero encapsulates chemical runoff.

03 // Chemical Engineering & Mechanics

Advanced Nano-Reinforcement Architecture

Combining 3D isotropic crumpled graphene ball topology with peroxide-mediated covalent cross-linking for unprecedented mechanical durability.

TOPOLOGICAL REINFORCEMENT

3D Isotropic Crumpled Graphene Ball Architecture

Flat graphene sheets suffer from severe agglomeration and restacking inside rubber matrices, losing their mechanical advantage. Micro-Rubber Zero uses isotropic crumpled graphene balls (inspired by Northwestern University nanoscale aerosol processing).

  • Agglomeration Resistant: Spherical crumpled geometry prevents flat sheet stacking.
  • Omni-Directional Stress Dispersion: Isotropic structure dissipates high-shear braking and cornering forces uniformly.
  • Mechanical Interlocking: Polymer chains physically lock into paper-ball folds under high strain.
ISOTROPIC GRAPHENE TOPOLOGY

Eliminates 2D flat-sheet stacking for 360° mechanical energy absorption.

COVALENT BONDING

Peroxide-Mediated Covalent Cross-Linking (DCP)

Rather than relying solely on sulfur vulcanization, Dicumyl Peroxide (DCP) is added as an organic peroxide curing agent. Under thermal processing, DCP decomposes into reactive alkoxy radicals.

  • Free Radical Generation: Thermal curing splits DCP to extract hydrogen atoms from rubber backbones.
  • Direct Carbon-Carbon (C-C) Bonds: Creates covalent linkages directly between the synthetic elastomer polymer chains and functionalized graphene sites.
  • Thermal Resilience: C-C bonds possess higher bond energy than sulfur-sulfur bonds, preventing thermal breakdown.
C-C COVALENT CROSS-LINKING

DCP radicals bind rubber backbones to graphene nanoplatelets.
